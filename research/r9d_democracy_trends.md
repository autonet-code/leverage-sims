# r9d: Democracy as a function of leverage, erosion speed, and 2024-2026 trend priors

Date: 2026-09-30. Quality: H = measured data, M = solid literature / partial data, L = judgment.
Web search budget was exhausted this session, so sources are the documents fetched directly (V-Dem DR2026 PDF, IEP GPI 2026, GTI 2026, ETR 2025, PRIO, ACLED, FAO, PRRI, Brennan) plus standard literature cited from memory (marked [lit]).

## 1. Theory: democracy tracks popular leverage
- Acemoglu & Robinson (2000, 2006) [lit]: elites concede franchise/redistribution when the revolution threat is credible and repression is costly. Remove the threat, remove the concession. M/H
- Boix (2003) [lit]: democracy survives when the rich lose less from it than from repression (low inequality, mobile assets). AI capital is highly mobile, but extreme inequality raises the stakes of redistribution: ambiguous sign. M
- Scheve & Stasavage (2016) [lit]: top tax rates rose mainly in countries that mass-mobilized for war (compensatory argument). Mass armies are the leverage; drone/autonomous militaries remove it. M/H
- Tilly (1990) [lit]: rights were bargained in exchange for taxes and soldiers. Both inputs shrink as labor share and human military share fall. M
- Przeworski et al. (2000) [lit]: no democracy above ~$6k (1985 PPP) GDP pc ever broke down to 2000. This income shield is now visibly failing (Hungary, US 2025). The shield was plausibly leverage (educated, employed, organized citizens), not income as such. M
- Rentier evidence (see m4): Ross ~0.5-0.67x transition odds, Haber-Menaldo null. Effect real but contested. M

Model implication: democracy should be a state variable whose erosion hazard rises as leverage L(t) falls, L = weighted mix of (economic need for mass labor, credible revolt capacity, human share of coercive force, tax share from individuals). No structural bar on harsh options for formal democracies.

## 2. Erosion speed (V-Dem Democracy Report 2026, fetched) H
- 44 autocratizing countries (2005: 12); 18 democratizing (2005: 27); 10 new autocratizers in 2025 incl. UK, Italy, Croatia, Slovakia, Slovenia.
- 74% of world population in autocracies (2005: 50%); 41% live in autocratizing countries (2005: 9%); 7% in liberal democracies. 92 autocracies vs 87 democracies.
- Average citizen's democracy level back to 1978.
- USA: lost liberal democracy status; LDI change -0.218 since 2023 episode start, mostly in 2025. "Only six countries register larger one-year drops on the LDI since 2000," five were military coups, one a self-coup; only 35 larger one-year drops (0.14%) since 1789. "Typically, autocratizing countries of the third wave have taken about a decade to make such a deep dive." Legislative constraints lost one-third of value in 2025 (lowest in 100+ yrs). Electoral components "stable, for now."
- Tactics: media censorship in 73% of autocratizers, civil-society repression 68%, torture substantially increasing in 33 countries.
- Democratization U-turn success once begun: 70% (so erosion is reversible when leverage remains).
- Boese et al. 2021 [lit, not re-fetched]: democracies often avoid onset, but most that enter an episode end in breakdown (roughly 2/3 to 4/5). L/M.
- US: 52 national emergencies in effect (Brennan, Sep 2026). Emergency-powers use is chronic, not rare. M

## 3. Violence and extremism trends
- US political violence at highest since 1970s (GPI 2026; ~300 acts Jan 2021-Nov 2024; first half 2025 ~150 attacks, ~2x same period 2024). US political instability indicator -38.5% in one year, largest in GPI history; US ranks 134th; 85% of Americans think political violence is rising. M/H
- High-profile: two Trump assassination attempts (2024), Minnesota legislators shot (Jun 2025), Charlie Kirk killed (Sep 2025); anti-AI targeting: Altman home Molotov (2026), anti-data-center extremism warning (Soufan, May 2026) [from m4]. H
- Attitudes: PRRI "patriots may have to resort to violence" 15% (2021) -> 23% (2023) (R 33%, I 22%, D 13%); ~20% of 18-29 say political violence sometimes justified (Sep 2025). H for attitudes; attitude-to-action conversion is tiny. 
- GTI 2026: global terrorism deaths -28% to 5,582, but West +280% to 57; youth 42% of terror investigations in Europe/NA (3x since 2021); radicalization now takes weeks; lone actors 93% of fatal Western attacks. H

## 4. Armed conflict (record levels) H
- 61 state-based conflicts in 2024, most since 1946 (PRIO/UCDP); GPI 2026 also counts 61, and 103 countries in external conflicts (59 in 2008).
- Internal conflict deaths ~181,000 in 2025 (peak 309,000 in 2023; 29,000 in 2008). Countries with >=1,000 conflict deaths: 8 (2008) -> 20. ACLED: 204,605 events and >240,000 deaths Dec 2024-Nov 2025, "steady."
- Global peacefulness down 12 consecutive years; GPI says "conditions that precede conflict are the worst since WWII." Military spending record $2.9T (2025), 10th straight rise; rose in 126 countries.
- Military automation: drone attacks +11,500% 2018-2025 (565 armed groups); target-to-fire from ~1 day (1990s) to 5 s (Russian V2U autonomous selection); Lavender flagged 37,000 targets with ~20 s human review at ~10% error; Maven contract ceiling +165% in 12 months. H/M

## 5. Moral cost of killing
- Sagan & Valentino (2017) [lit]: ~60% of Americans approved a hypothetical nuclear strike killing 2M Iranian civilians to save 20,000 US soldiers. Outgroup civilian life is cheap in wartime framing. M
- Partisan dehumanization: 2/3 of Republicans, half of Democrats call opponents "downright evil" (2021). M
- AI targeting compresses human review to seconds: moral friction per killing is being engineered out in live wars (Lavender, V2U). H (fact), L (magnitude for domestic use)
- Historical: mass killing is far likelier during war/emergency (Harff 2003; Valentino 2004 [lit]); most genocides occur inside wars. M/H

## 6. Ecological stress H/M
- ETR 2025: conflict death rates 50% higher where water stress rises through rainfall seasonality; ~4x conflict deaths in severe-seasonality areas; seasonality increasing across >60% of covered areas; no modern interstate water wars (treaties work). Ecology is a risk multiplier, not a core driver.
- FAO food price index 133.3 (Aug 2026), +2.5% y/y, 17% below the Mar 2022 peak: elevated, not crisis.
- Forced displacement ~117-123M (UNHCR 2025) [lit, not re-fetched]. M

## 7. Depopulation / successionist ideology among elites: L
- Dominant elite tech ideology is pronatalist ("population collapse"), not depopulationist. Degrowth/anti-natalism is academic/activist with no power base. Eco-fascist "overpopulation" motifs appear in some mass-shooter manifestos (Christchurch, El Paso 2019): fringe but lethal.
- AI successionism (hand the future to AI even if humans go extinct): Richard Sutton, Daniel Faggella, reportedly Larry Page ("speciesist" exchange as reported by Musk), parts of e/acc. Described as gaining ground among technologists; no prevalence survey exists. This is the closest existing ideology to a depopulation justification for a closed-loop elite, and it is about replacement by AI, not by an elite human group.
- No evidence of an explicit "reduce the human population" program at any powerful actor today. Prior should start low and rise mainly conditional on (a) closure, (b) war/emergency, (c) successionist or eco-framing gaining a patron.

## 9. Findings for the model
1. Democracy is not a fixed type. V-Dem shows established democracies (US, UK, Italy) autocratizing now, and the US moved at coup speed without a coup while labor leverage was still high. So erosion does not even require the loss of leverage; loss of leverage removes the main recovery channel (U-turns succeed 70% when citizens still matter).
2. Encode democracy as D(t) in [0,1] with erosion hazard h = h0 * M^(1-L(t)) (h0 ~0.5%/yr, M ~5, range 2-15) plus shocks from emergencies/war, and recovery rate proportional to L(t). Harsh options become available as D falls, with no structural bar.
3. Trend priors all point the same way but at modest rates: conflict (record counts, 6x deaths since 2008), political violence (US highest since 1970s, doubling y/y), autocratization (+1pp world pop/yr), military spending (+6%/yr), AI-targeted killing with seconds of human review. Ecology and food are multipliers, currently moderate.
4. Grievance is not yet violent at scale: anti-AI protest is mostly permitting fights; attitudinal support (~23%) converts to action at ~1%. A hostile faction at 0.5% of population is still millions of people in large countries, and lone-actor violence is the dominant form (93% of fatal Western attacks), which is hard to deter and easy to use as justification for repression.
5. Depopulation ideology is the weakest-evidence link: no powerful actor holds it today; elite tech is mostly pronatalist; successionism (replacement by AI) is the live variant. Keep the unconditional prior low and let it rise with war, emergency and closure rather than hard-coding it high or zero.
6. Counter-evidence to keep: U-turns (Brazil, Poland) succeed; no modern water wars; terrorism deaths globally fell 28%; conflict deaths are below the 2023 peak; electoral components in the US remain stable so far.
