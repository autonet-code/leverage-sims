# r8b: Elite dependence on labor, history, and 2026 automation economics

Date: 2026-09-30. Evidence quality: H = strong data, M = moderate, L = weak/judgment.

## 1. Cases where elite dependence on a population's labor fell
| Case | What happened to the superfluous group | Excess mortality | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| English enclosures (16th-19th c.) | Eviction, proletarianization, migration to towns/colonies | ~0 direct | M |
| Highland Clearances (1750-1860) | Tens of thousands evicted for sheep; emigration | Low (famine 1846-56 locally) | M |
| Irish famine 1845-52 | Laissez-faire relief, food exports continued, evictions (Gregory clause) | ~1M of 8.5M (~12%), plus 1-2M emigrated; pop 8.2M -> 4.4M by 1911 | H |
| Congo Free State 1885-1908 | Labor NEEDED (rubber) yet atrocities; pop loss est. 20-50% (contested) | High | M/L |
| Post-abolition US South | Labor still needed: sharecropping, convict leasing, Jim Crow, terror | Moderate (lynching ~4,000) | H |
| Apartheid homelands 1960-83 | ~3.5M forcibly removed; "surplus people" (elderly, women, children, unemployed) dumped in Bantustans | Elevated infant mortality in camps; no extermination | H |
| Soviet Kazakh famine 1930-33 | Nomads collectivized; ~1.5M dead (~38% of Kazakhs) | Very high; ideology + extraction, not "uselessness" | M |
| Nazi Aktion T4 | "Useless eaters" (disabled): ~70k gassed by Aug 1941, ~200-300k total | Near-total for targeted | H |
| Gulf rentier states | Citizen labor unneeded (citizens ~10-12% of UAE/Qatar pop); citizens bought off with public jobs and transfers; migrants under kafala | Citizens: none. Migrants: elevated worker deaths | H |
| Resource curse (Ross) | Oil reduces democratization, raises repression capacity, but elites also pay patronage | Low on average | M |
| US horses | 26.5M (1915) -> 3.1M (1960), -88%; tractor/horse power crossover 1945 | Decline mostly via no breeding, some slaughter | H (data), L (analog) |

Pattern: when a group becomes superfluous, the modal outcome is removal, neglect, and demographic decline (emigration, lower fertility), not extermination. Mass death occurs mostly when combined with ideology, famine, or war. Deliberate killing justified by economic uselessness (T4) is rare and ideological. Brutality also occurs when labor IS needed (Congo, slavery), so dependence is not the only protection; what protects is leverage (votes, revolt, soldiers, emigration options).

## 2. Counter-cases (maintenance of non-productive people)
- Modern welfare states support ~half their population outside the labor force (children, retirees, disabled). OECD public social spending ~21% GDP, pensions ~8%. Driven by votes, not labor need. H
- Franchise extension as revolution-avoidance (Acemoglu & Robinson 2000); progressive taxation driven by mass-mobilization warfare (Scheve & Stasavage 2016). Both show concessions track mass leverage. This SUPPORTS the scenario's mechanism: remove leverage, remove the reason for concessions. M/H
- Gulf rentiers maintain economically useless citizens generously (legitimacy, security still staffed partly by citizens). H
- Black Death raised wages via labor scarcity: leverage theory again. H

## 3. Elite attitudes
- Page, Bartels & Seawright 2013 (wealthy Chicagoans, n~83): top-wealth respondents far less supportive of government job guarantees (~19% vs ~68% public) and income floors. M
- Gilens & Page 2014: policy tracks affluent preferences. M (contested)
- Historical "utility of poverty" doctrine (Mandeville, mercantilists): explicit instrumental view of workers. M
- Paternalism, noblesse oblige, philanthropy, and tech-elite UBI talk also exist. "Disdain" is too uniform; "instrumental indifference constrained by leverage" fits better. M/L

## 4. 2026 automation economics
- Top-4 hyperscaler capex 2026: ~$700-765B (+~77% on 2025); 2027 forecasts ~$1T (Goldman). H
- Lab revenue: Anthropic run rate ~$65B (Jul/Aug 2026), OpenAI ~$40B; OpenAI projects $14B 2026 loss, breakeven ~2030. Revenue/capex ~0.15. Bubble/correction risk real. M/H
- US firm AI use (Census BTOS): 17% (Nov 2025) -> 23% (Aug 2026); ~32-40% employment-weighted; Fed: 78% of workers at firms using AI. H
- Stanford/ADP: age 22-25 employment in most-exposed jobs down ~11% since Nov 2022; gap vs unexposed 19% by Jun 2026; via hiring freezes, not layoffs; no broad displacement yet. H
- GDPval (OpenAI, Sep 2025): best models win/tie experts on ~48% of tasks; ~100x cheaper/faster on inference-only basis (excludes review/integration). M
- MIT (Thompson et al. 2024): only ~23% of vision-task wage bill economically attractive to automate at then-current costs. M (dated by fast cost declines)
- Industrial robots: 542k installed 2024, >500k/yr four straight years, 2x in 10 years (~7%/yr). Not currently exponential in the fast sense. H

## 5. Delays of profitable technology
- Electric dynamo ~40 yrs to productivity payoff (David 1990); tractors: first Ford 1917, power crossover 1945 (~28 yrs). H
- Local blocks: ILA port contract bars full automation through ~2030; taxi/ride-hail bans; nuclear. These delay 5-20 years locally; global competition erodes them. M
- GenAI adoption by individuals faster than PC/internet (Bick et al. 2024). H

## Verdicts
Claim 1 (elites tolerated workers because necessary; calculus shifts when not needed): PARTLY HOLDS. The leverage mechanism is well supported (franchise, welfare, wages track mass bargaining power). "Universal disdain" overstated. "First time in history" holds for whole populations vs a closed-loop actor; partial precedents (rentier states, homelands, Irish) show outcomes range from generous buy-off to neglect and removal; extermination is the tail, not the mode. Key last check historically: rulers need humans to staff coercion; robotics removing that is genuinely unprecedented.
Claim 2 (profitable automation WILL be done): MOSTLY HOLDS in aggregate and over time, with lags of 5-30 years, local blocks, and the caveat that AI profitability at scale is not yet proven (capex ~6x lab revenue). Global competition makes durable blocking unlikely. "Will be done" is right; "soon" is the contested part.
