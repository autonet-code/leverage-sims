# m5: Depopulation (S5) and Paranoid Singleton (S6), research priors

Research agent output, 2026-09-30. Scope: base rates and conditions for regime-led mass killing, famine/neglect as policy, purges and elite self-destruction in personalist regimes, selectorate logic. These feed the S5 and S6 nodes, which are *conditional on* S3/S4 (a small AI-controlling elite with automated coercion and an economically irrelevant public).

Honesty note: no historical case matches the S5/S6 setup (an elite that needs neither the public's labor nor its soldiers). Everything below is analogy. Parameters marked "judgment" are my estimates from the analogs, not published numbers.

## 1. Base rates

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| New state-led mass killing onsets | about 1.4% of countries per year (about 2/yr globally); mean 2-yr model risk 1.6% | Early Warning Project (USHMM) Statistical Risk Assessment 2024-25 |
| Mass-atrocity episodes 1900-2014 (at least 1,000 civilians killed by governments) | just over 200 (about 2/yr), at least 100M deaths (conservative) | Anderton & Brauer, *JEL* 2021 / *Economic Aspects of Genocides* 2016 |
| 20th-century mass killing deaths | 60M-150M (Valentino, at least 50k intentional deaths in 5 yrs); 262M democide (Rummel, upper bound, widely seen as high) | Valentino, *Final Solutions* (2004); Rummel, *Death by Government* |
| Share of state failures (internal war / regime collapse) that became geno/politicide, 1955-97 | 35 of 126 = 28% | Harff, *APSR* 2003 |
| Harff's six risk factors | prior genocide, political upheaval, exclusionary ideology + autocracy, ethnic elite / cleavage, low development, low trade openness (74% classification accuracy) | Harff 2003 |

Valentino's key finding, the one most relevant here: mass killing starts with **a small group of powerful leaders pursuing a strategic goal**, often without broad societal support, and needs only a small number of perpetrators. Ethnic hatred and regime type matter less than commonly assumed. That cuts both ways for S5. It makes elite-driven killing plausible without popular buy-in. But historically the motive is almost always a perceived *threat* (guerrillas, class enemies, rival ethnic groups), not mere uselessness.

## 2. Deliberate famine and neglect as policy

| Case | Deaths | Share of pop. | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Holodomor, Ukraine 1932-33 | 3.5-5M | about 12-17% of Ukrainian SSR | grain requisition quotas, border closure, blacklisting villages |
| Great Leap Forward, China 1959-61 | 15-45M (Yang Jisheng 36M, Dikotter 45M) | about 2-7% | procurement over subsistence; information suppression; officials purged for reporting shortfalls |
| Khmer Rouge 1975-79 | 1.5-2.0M | about 20-25% (highest known own-population share) | forced relocation, starvation, execution |
| Post-Soviet mortality crisis 1990s | about 2.5-4M excess deaths | about 2% | neglect and collapse, not intent (Brainerd; Cornia & Paniccia) |
| Bengal 1943 | 2-3M | n/a | colonial wartime priority, not extermination intent |

Pattern: large policy-driven famines happen where the rulers do not answer to the victims (Sen: no major famine in a functioning democracy with a free press). Neglect deaths (Bengal, 1990s Russia) are as large as intentional ones and need no one to decide to kill. For S5 the neglect path looks more likely than the extermination path.

## 3. Labor irrelevance and rentier logic

- Rentier states (Ross, *The Oil Curse*; Kulveit et al. "Gradual Disempowerment", ICML 2025) govern with less accountability when revenue does not depend on citizens. That is the S4 logic.
- Counter-evidence for S5: rich rentier autocracies (Kuwait, Qatar, UAE, Saudi Arabia) typically **buy quiescence with transfers** and import labor. They do not exterminate surplus citizens. Their repression is targeted, not demographic. When the revenue is large relative to the cost of feeding people (as AI output would be), co-optation is cheap.
- Settler-colonial eliminations (Herero 1904-08, about 50-80% of Herero killed; Tasmania; parts of the Americas) are the closest match to "people not needed for labor, land wanted". Wolfe's "logic of elimination" contrasts these with labor-extraction colonies, where the native population was exploited, not removed. This is the strongest historical support for the manifesto's S5, but the victims were out-groups, not the rulers' own citizens.

## 4. Purges, personalism, and elites turning on each other (S6)

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Autocrats removed non-constitutionally by **regime insiders** (vs uprisings) 1946-2008 | 205 of 316 (about 65%); popular uprisings about 32 | Svolik, *The Politics of Authoritarian Rule* (2012) |
| Leaders punished (exile/prison/death) within 1 yr of exit, all leaders | about 25% | Goemans, Gleditsch & Chiozza, Archigos (JPR 2009) |
| Same, leaders removed irregularly by domestic forces | about 80% punished; 43% exiled | Archigos |
| Stalin: 17th Congress Central Committee (1934) arrested by 1939 | 98 of 139 (70%), most shot | Soviet archival figures (Khrushchev 1956 speech) |
| Stalin: 17th Congress delegates arrested | 1,108 of 1,966 (56%) | same |
| Great Terror 1937-38 | about 1.5M arrested, about 680k executed | NKVD records (Getty, Rittersporn, Zemskov) |
| Perpetrators purged: NKVD chiefs | Yagoda shot 1938, Yezhov shot 1940, Beria shot 1953 | standard histories |
| Khmer Rouge S-21 (mostly party cadres) | about 14k-20k prisoners; 2,404 (1975-76), 6,330 (1977), 5,765 (H1 1978) | USHMM; DC-Cam |
| Saddam 1979 Ba'ath "Comrades' Hall" purge | 68 named, 22 executed | standard histories |
| Personalist share of autocracies | about 23% (1988) to about 40% (2010s) | Geddes, Wright & Frantz; Kendall-Taylor et al. 2016 |
| Coup attempts 1950-2010 | about 457, about half succeeded | Powell & Thyne, JPR 2011 |

Selectorate theory (Bueno de Mesquita et al. 2003, *The Logic of Political Survival*): as the winning coalition W shrinks, rulers shift from public goods to private payoffs for the coalition, and loyalty rests on members' fear of exclusion. An AI-enabled regime is the limit case: W is close to 0 humans once coercion and production are automated (Davidson, Finnveden & Hadshar, "AI-Enabled Coups", Forethought 2025). The manifesto's "one person with rights" is the W=1 endpoint. Theory predicts almost no public goods. It does not by itself predict killing, since a public that poses no threat and costs almost nothing is not obviously worth eliminating.

Mechanism check on "guilt turns to paranoia": the historical driver of perpetrator-on-perpetrator purges is **commitment problems and fear of betrayal** (Svolik), plus scapegoating the executors (Yezhov), rather than guilt. The outcome still matches the manifesto: inner circles of terror regimes are purged at high rates.

## 6. Elites after atrocity: case tally (for param 12)

Turned on itself: USSR (Yagoda, Yezhov, then Beria after Stalin), China (Liu Shaoqi, Lin Biao 1971, Gang of Four arrested within a month of Mao's death), Cambodia (S-21 cadre purges, Eastern Zone 1978, Pol Pot had Son Sen killed 1997 and was then tried by his own movement), Iraq (1979 and after), Ethiopia Derg (Mengistu killed rivals), North Korea (Jang Song-thaek 2013), Syria (Rifaat exiled after Hama). Did not: Indonesia 1965 (Suharto stable 32 yrs), Guatemala, Argentina (later prosecuted by democracy), Rwanda and Nazi Germany (ended by outside force first). Roughly 7 of 12, about 60%.

How mass-killing regimes ended: mostly external defeat (Nazis, Khmer Rouge via Vietnam, Rwanda via RPF) or the leader's natural death followed by an insider purge (Stalin, Mao). Popular uprising was rare. That supports the manifesto's claim that the public is not the threat. The threat comes from rivals and other states, which is the S7 logic.

## 7. Implications for the model

- S6 (paranoid consolidation) has **stronger** historical support than S5 (depopulation). Insider purges and personalization are the modal dynamics of unchecked autocracy.
- S5 as *active extermination of one's own economically useless public* has no clean precedent. Precedents exist for elimination of out-groups when land or security was at stake (settler colonialism, Khmer Rouge class enemies) and for mass death by indifference (famines). Rentier states mostly pay off surplus populations instead. Model S5 as a split: active depopulation (param 6) vs neglect/fertility decline (param 7) vs managed dependency/UBI (param 8).
- Automated coercion removes the historical brake of perpetrator scarcity and defection, which raises param 6 relative to history. Near-zero cost of feeding people under AI abundance lowers it. I treat these as roughly offsetting, hence the wide range.
